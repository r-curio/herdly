# Notch placement and keyboard interaction — issue #12

Status: implemented and checked in the built app on a single built-in notch display. Agreed behavior is in [product.md](product.md); the pure rules live in `Sources/HerdlyCore/NotchPlacement.swift`. The two-display and display-disconnect cases are covered by focused tests and by the operator walkthrough below, which has not yet been recorded on a second display.

Revision (hover-expand notch): the panel is one black shape with concave ears at its top corners. On a display with a camera housing the collapsed strip is the housing row itself: exactly the housing height, anchored to the top edge of the screen, with a 112-point wing left of the housing for one sprite per session (at most three, in priority order, each in a color that stays with the session, stepping while that session works; the unlit sprite while no session is listed; further sessions appear only in the open queue) followed by the to-check count, which reads "N to check" beside a single sprite while the queue is open and is hidden at zero (`StripWing`; D2, D3, D22, #318), and a 60-point wing right of it for the usage rings, one per source, Claude's ahead of Codex's (see [Codex usage limits](codex-usage.md) and [Claude usage](claude-usage.md)); open, that wing is first sized to fit each ring's numbers (`UsageStripReadouts.wingWidth(_:expanded:)`, 148 points for both sources with both windows) and the open growth is then shared by both wings, so the housing stays centred; the name is read in the open queue, not the strip (`NotchPlacement.strip`); on any other display the strip is inline with the menu bar row that is actually showing there (as tall as the bar, anchored to the top edge, centered where the bar is empty), or 40 points tall at the top edge when that display has no menu bar (a full-screen app, or an auto-hidden bar); the menu bar height comes from the window server's menu bar window on that display (`NotchDisplay.shownMenuBarHeight`), not from `visibleFrame`, which reserves the strip whether or not the bar is showing. The collapsed strip shows the sprites, the to-check badge (`StripSummary`), and the usage limits (`CodexUsageReading`, then the newest current `ClaudeUsageReading`). Hover expands it (`HoverHold` keeps it open for 0.3 s after the pointer leaves); a new completion or request opens only its own card for the card-open time, 3 seconds by default (`CardOpenDuration`), and the open panel is exactly as tall as its measured content, capped at 480 points, and 60 points wider than the strip with its right wing at the open usage width, half on each side, never narrower than 380 points, so hovering visibly widens it whatever usage rings are shown (`NotchPlacement.expandedGrowth`); the per-profile Wide and Extra wide panel widths grow it by 160 or 260 points instead, never narrower than 480 or 580 points, and never past 16 points from either side of the display (`PanelWidth`). Older statements below about a margin below the housing, a 210-point collapsed width, a 380-point expanded width, click-to-expand, or whole-queue popups are superseded by this revision; the display-selection, freeze-while-interacting, Escape, and accessibility-label rules stand.

## One panel, one queue

Herdly creates exactly one `NotchPanel` and one `AttentionQueue`. Every display change re-runs placement of that same panel; nothing is duplicated per display, and no queue state is keyed by display. The panel is configured to join all Spaces as a full-screen auxiliary window at status-bar level; the probe records those flags, and whether it actually appears over full-screen apps and on every Space is walkthrough step 10, not yet verified live.

## Active-display rule

`NotchDisplaySelection.choose(displays:pointer:current:interacting:preference:)` decides where the panel goes on every refresh (five times a second while a card or the queue is showing, the pointer or keyboard is on the notch, or an approval is actionable; once a second otherwise (`RefreshCadence`, #301); and on app activation or screen-parameter changes):

1. **Interacting → freeze.** While the pointer is over the panel, or the panel has keyboard focus while unsent text is in one of its fields or VoiceOver is running (#376), or the hotkey's queue holds the keyboard (#427), keep the display the panel is already on. A spotlight card held by keyboard focus (#428) does not freeze placement by itself. Controls never move to another display mid-click or mid-Tab; expanding or collapsing still resizes the panel in place. The app confirms the hover flag against the real pointer location, so a panel re-placed out from under the pointer does not stay frozen.
2. **Pinned → the pinned display.** With a pin (see [Pinned display](#pinned-display-2026-10-08)), the pinned display wherever the pointer is; while it is not attached, the built-in display. With neither attached, the steps below apply.
3. **Otherwise the display under the pointer.** This is the display the user is actively using.
4. **Pointer on no display → keep the current display.**
5. **No current display → the first attached display.**

A display that has disconnected is never chosen, even while interacting: the rule falls through to the pointer, then the first display. `NSApplication.didChangeScreenParametersNotification` triggers a refresh, so connect, disconnect, and rearrangement re-place the panel within one refresh.

## Geometry

`NotchPlacement.frame(on:expanded:rows:extraRows:)` is pure. On a display with a physical camera housing (`safeAreaInsets.top` > 0) the panel's top edge sits 6 pt below the housing; on a display without one it sits 12 pt below the top edge as a compact top-center panel. Width is 210 pt collapsed and 380 pt expanded; expanded height grows 65 pt per row from 150 pt and is capped at 380 pt, with the row list scrolling beyond that.

## Pinned display (2026-10-08)

The user can keep the notch on one display instead of following the pointer. The default is Follow pointer, so nothing changes until a display is chosen.

- **Choosing.** The Notch page of the controls window has a Show notch on picker in its Display section, and the S menu bar item has a Show notch on submenu. Both list Follow pointer, then each attached display by its macOS name, left to right (`NotchDisplayChoices`). Two displays with the same name are numbered in that order. Choosing an option re-places the panel at once; the queue, presentations, and approval deadlines are untouched.
- **Identity.** A pin is the display's UUID (`CGDisplayCreateUUIDFromDisplayID`), which survives reboots and reconnects where the display number does not, plus the label shown when it was chosen. Both are stored in UserDefaults as `notchDisplayPinID` and `notchDisplayPinName`; a missing or empty identifier means Follow pointer. A display the system reports no UUID for is not offered.
- **Pinned display not connected.** The notch shows on the built-in display, or follows the pointer when there is no built-in display (a closed lid). The pin is kept: the pickers list it as "*name* (not connected)", and the notch returns to it when it reconnects. The Settings picker's VoiceOver hint says which of these applies, naming the display as the picker does; the menu bar submenu lists only the choices.
- **Unchanged rules.** Interaction still freezes placement first, so a pinned display that reconnects mid-click does not pull the panel away until the interaction ends. A pinned external monitor showing no menu bar still tucks the strip, and only the pointer at that display's top edge reveals it; a new item's spotlight still slides it in.
- **Probe.** `--placement-probe` records each display's `persistent_id`, `name`, and `built_in`, and the `display_preference`. Launch arguments `-notchDisplayPinID <uuid> -notchDisplayPinName <name>` set a pin for that launch only through the UserDefaults argument domain, without saving anything.

## Interaction and timers

`NotchInteraction.isActive(pointerInside:keyboardFocus:)` is the single definition of "the user is interacting". It freezes placement and keeps the active single-item spotlight open; passive hover does not replace that spotlight with the full queue. Header activation explicitly opens the queue. Interaction does not touch `AttentionRequest.expiresAt`: the approval handoff deadline is absolute from companion receipt and `AttentionQueue.expire(at:)` ignores presentation entirely. `anApprovalHeldOpenUntilIActStillHandsBackAtExactlyThirtySeconds` holds an approval's card open through 30 s of interaction under Until I act while the request hands back at exactly 30 s. Outside interaction, a new card closes after the card-open time (3 seconds by default, `CardOpenDuration`); under Until I act it closes when the user's interaction with it ends.

## Keyboard and accessibility

- **Automatic notifications never take input.** Presentation uses `orderFrontRegardless`, which shows the panel without making it key; the panel is a non-activating panel, so Herdly itself never activates. The probe below records `is_key_after_presentation: false` with the panel visible.
- **Deliberate focus.** Clicking the panel gives it key status without activating Herdly. Focus from a click alone does not keep the queue open (#376, agreed 2026-11-03): once the pointer leaves and the hover grace ends, the panel folds and gives up key status so typing returns to the previous app, unless unsent text is in a field on screen or VoiceOver is running. A spotlight card is the exception (#428, agreed 2026-11-13): focus from a click or the hotkey holds it open, below. Tab from another app cannot reach a non-activating panel of another process, so keyboard-only entry is the hotkey below. Once the panel is key, Tab and Shift-Tab move between the header button, Approve, Deny, Open, Dismiss, Show full command, and Show full request; the expanded list is a focus section. Buttons use the standard bordered style so macOS draws its focus ring; the plain-style header button draws its own visible ring while focused.
- **Hotkey (#427, agreed 2026-11-13).** ⌃⌥Space by default (Settings › Notch › Keyboard: a recorder and an off switch, saved in UserDefaults as `notchHotkey.*` with the switch as a Bool, re-registered at once). `NotchHotkeyRegistrar` registers it with Carbon `RegisterEventHotKey` and `kEventHotKeyExclusive`, which needs no Accessibility permission and sees only that shortcut; Herdly installs no event tap. `eventHotKeyExistsErr` shows "Another app already uses this shortcut…" under the row and the notch stays click-only; any other failure shows its OSStatus. A recorded shortcut must hold Control, Option, or Command (`NotchHotkey`), so plain typing is never taken; `NotchHotkeyRecording` decides each press in the recorder (Escape alone cancels, a key without those modifiers is ignored). On a press, `NotchKeyboardEntry.press` hands back when the panel already has deliberate focus; otherwise Herdly remembers the frontmost app (`NotchKeyboardReturn`), and `NotchPanel.takeKeyboardFromHotkey` makes the panel key as a click does, without activating Herdly (only if the window server refuses key status to the inactive app is Herdly activated). `NotchKeyboardEntry.target` sends the keyboard to the card on screen (a spotlight or a clicked card) or opens the queue with `NotchPresentationController.openQueueForKeyboard()` and selects its top row. That queue, and a card opened from it with Return, is held while `update(keyboardFocus:)` stays true and freezes display placement; it shows the foot line "↑↓ select  ⏎ open  esc close  ⌃⌥Space hide". A clicked queue is not held by focus (#376). Approval and Claude question holds read the pointer only, so the hotkey pauses no countdown (ADR 0020).
- **Spotlight question by keyboard (#428, agreed 2026-11-13; ADR 0023 amendment).** A spotlight card holds open while the panel has deliberate keyboard focus, however long the user takes, until it hands back, and its card-open time starts over once focus leaves (`NotchPresentationController.update(keyboardFocus:)`); a fresh card waits behind it (`acceptsAgentSpotlight(keyboardFocus:)`) and is named in one dim line until Send or Escape ends the hold, its own deadline running. When the hotkey enters a card, the panel itself becomes first responder (`makeFirstResponder(nil)`), so keys reach `NotchPanel.keyDown` rather than the typed field, and VoiceOver announces the question. On a spotlight question card with the keyboard (`Companion.keyboardCard`, `SpotlightKeyboardCard`) 1–9 select, Return does Next or Send while enabled, O and X do Open and Dismiss, ← → step questions, and Escape folds; every key goes through `SpotlightCardKeys`, so nothing sends except Return on a card Herdly can still answer. Keys are mapped by one table for cards with and without the keyboard (`SpotlightCardFocus`), and O opens only where the card shows Open. A Claude countdown that ends while focused hands back with no answer, and the keyboard hold ends (`update(keyboardHoldEnded:)`): the card follows its normal open time as if focus had just left, digits stay inert and Return opens Claude while it shows, and when it folds the keyboard goes back through `handBackKeyboard()`. After a send the route records, the card shows "Sent to <agent>: …" for the whole second even if its question leaves the queue meanwhile (`keepsSentCard`), then the panel folds and gives the keyboard back. The foot line names the app `NotchKeyboardReturn.target` would return to. VoiceOver announces the question on screen whenever a card takes the keyboard, by the hotkey or a click.
- **Clicked question card by keyboard (#438, agreed 2026-11-13).** A question card opened from the queue (a click on its row, or Return on the hotkey queue's selected row) takes the same keys and key labels while the panel has deliberate keyboard focus: `Companion.updateClickedKeyboardCard` sets `clickedKeyboardCard` from `SpotlightCardFocus.clickedWithKeyboard(answerable:)`, the keyboard row of `SpotlightCardKeys` (answering while Herdly can answer it, read-only otherwise). It runs on every refresh, when a click gives the panel deliberate focus (`NotchPanel.onDeliberateKeyboardFocus`), and before every key, and both the labels and `pressQuestionCardKey` read `Companion.answerKeysCard` (the keyboard spotlight card or this one), so a key never acts on a state its labels do not show. Only Return reaches Next or Send, only while enabled; on a read-only card (the Codex app without the daemon, or a Claude question whose countdown ended) digits are swallowed, Return and O open the agent only where Open is shown, X dismisses, and ← → step. In the typed-answer field (Tab), keys are text. None of the spotlight's hold, wait line, foot line, sent line or ring applies: Send returns to the queue (D10), Escape steps back to the queue (D6), and focus pauses no countdown (ADR 0020).
- **Approval card by keyboard (#439, agreed 2026-11-13).** An approval card with deliberate keyboard focus (a clicked card, the hotkey's queue then Return, or a spotlight the hotkey or a click gave the keyboard) takes ⌘⏎ for Approve and ⌘⌫ for Deny, and O for Open. `NotchPanel.keyDown` sends ⌘⏎ (Return or keypad Enter with Command only), ⌘⌫ (Delete with Command only), bare Delete, Return, digits and letters to `Companion.pressApprovalCardKey`, which reads the card as drawn now (`ApprovalCardReading` with `canDecide`) and maps the key through `ApprovalCardKeys` (HerdlyCore); a key the table gives no action goes on to AppKit. Only ⌘⏎ and ⌘⌫ decide, only while Approve and Deny are shown and enabled; the card then calls `decideAgent` exactly as a click does, which checks the request again, deadline included. Bare Return, other letters, digits and bare Delete never decide. Past the deadline, handed back, or on a request that stays native, O or Return opens where the card shows Open. `Companion.updateApprovalKeysCard` sets `approvalKeysCard` on every refresh and before every key, and the key labels read it, so a label never shows a key that does not act. Focus pauses no deadline: `applyApprovalHolds` reads the pointer only (ADR 0020).
- **Hand back.** The hotkey again, Escape at the top level, and the panel folding for any reason all go through `Companion.handBackKeyboard()`: a key panel is ordered out and `NotchKeyboardReturn.handBack` picks what to activate. After a hotkey entry it re-activates the remembered app while it still runs (matched by process identifier and bundle identifier); if it has quit Herdly activates nothing and macOS chooses; if a third app came forward meanwhile (an Open from the notch) that app keeps the keyboard. After a click entry it re-activates the frontmost app as before. A panel that lost key status to another app forgets the remembered app, so nothing is pulled back later. Herdly never activates a terminal it guessed.
- **Escape.** `NotchPanel.cancelOperation` and a text field's Escape both go through `NotchPresentationController.escape()`: on a clicked card it returns to the queue (#312, D6); otherwise it collapses every presentation (`Attention.collapse()`, which never dismisses) and, if the panel was key, hands the keyboard back as above. The next refresh shows the collapsed panel again.
- **Row selection.** Up and Down arrows that no control handled reach `NotchPanel.keyDown` and move `QueueRowSelection` over the open queue's rows; Return does what a click on the selected row does (`QueueRowSelection.action`): an approval or question opens as its clicked card, a working row opens or closes in place (#319), and a result, waiting, or status-unknown row opens its session through the usual Open, after which the panel folds back (#322). On an unreachable result row Return is not handled.
- **Labels.** Each row is an accessibility container labeled with its kind and subject (task name when Codex provided one, otherwise the shortened session ID). Every button's label names its action and subject, for example "Approve this request once: deploy, session 01a0916c-337b"; Open reads "Open Session: <subject>" whatever its visible label ("Open", "Open in Codex", "Open in Claude"). The header reads the working and to-check counts and says whether the queue is expanded.
- **No running commentary.** The panel shows no navigation result line, and no keyboard hint except the hotkey's queue foot line above (the general hint was removed 2026-09-14 at the user's request); navigation results remain in the controls window, and the card's own status sentence reports what Herdly did for a question.
- **Long details.** Task, project, action, command, and reason wrap in full; an approval card's command past six lines scrolls in its box and Show full command lifts the cap (#320). The full request stays behind Show full request. A command is never shortened, and a shortened one elsewhere is never presented as the complete action.

Reported on an external 3440 × 1440 display with Chrome in full screen: the strip floated 30 pt below the top edge, over Chrome's tab bar, with its ears fused to nothing. `visibleFrame` still reserved the menu bar strip although no menu bar was showing. ## Hover at the top edge (2026-10-09)

Reported: with the pointer pushed against the top of the screen above the strip, the panel did not expand. Measured on the built-in display, the strip spans Cocoa y 950–982 and the pinned pointer reports y 982 (y 981 one point lower). `CGRect.contains` and AppKit's tracking areas, which drive SwiftUI's hover, both treat `maxY` as outside, so that row never counted as hover. The panel's top edge row now counts as over the panel by geometry (`NotchHover.pointerTouchesTopEdge`), and mouse-move monitors (which need no Accessibility permission) refresh as soon as the pointer reaches or leaves that row instead of waiting for the 0.2 s poll. `pointerPushedAgainstTheTopEdgeCountsAsOverThePanel` covers the rule. A live check of the expansion was not completed.

## Panel moved after display sleep (2026-10-13)

Reported: after the screen turned off and back on, the collapsed strip sometimes sat in the middle of the screen. While a display sleeps, wakes, or is reconfigured, the window server can move the panel itself; refresh only set the frame when the computed frame changed, and after wake it computed the same frame as before sleep, so the moved panel stayed put until something else changed the frame. Each refresh now also compares the panel's actual frame with the target and puts a drifted panel back at once (`NotchFrameSync`), ignoring differences up to 1 pt (backing-store rounding) and the in-between frames of a running resize animation. The next refresh corrects it within 0.2 s while the notch is active and within 1 s while it is idle (`RefreshCadence`, #301). `panelMovedByTheWindowServerIsPutBackWhenTheTargetIsUnchanged` and `frameSyncIgnoresRoundingAndRunningAnimationsButFollowsANewTarget` cover the rule. Display sleep cannot be triggered from the automated checks; a live check after waking the display has not yet been recorded.

## Boundaries

- "Actively using" means the display under the pointer, or the pinned display when the user has chosen one. Keyboard-only users on a multi-display setup get the pointer's display; a rule based on the key window's display is deferred until it is needed.
- The hotkey is the only global key; a shortcut another app registered first leaves the notch click-only. Herdly registers with `kEventHotKeyExclusive`, so Carbon answers `eventHotKeyExistsErr` when another process already holds the shortcut through `RegisterEventHotKey`, and Settings shows the taken line. A shortcut claimed another way (System Settings › Keyboard Shortcuts, or an app's event tap) is invisible to Carbon: it may register without error and never fire.
- `--assume-codex-away` and `--placement-probe` are lab flags compiled into the app like `--restart-report`; they change nothing unless passed.
- Focus rings depend on macOS Full Keyboard Access for Tab to reach buttons, as in any macOS app.
